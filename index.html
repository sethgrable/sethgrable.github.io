I am a theoretical physicist completing my PhD at the University of Colorado Boulder, where I work with Paul Romatschke on nonperturbative methods for non-Abelian gauge theories. I am moving that toolkit into machine learning research.

My published work finds analytic and semi-analytic solutions to strongly interacting quantum field theories in regimes where perturbation theory breaks down: the Yang–Mills vacuum beyond one-loop order, confinement in many-flavor QCD, and exactly solvable interacting models in 3+1 dimensions. First-author papers appear in JHEP and Physics Letters B, with further manuscripts under review at Nuclear Physics B and JHEP.

What draws me to machine learning is that the obstructions are the same ones. A nonperturbative field theory is hard because its action has flat directions and degenerate saddles, because the relevant physics lives at strong coupling, and because the system reorganizes itself across sharp phase transitions. Neural network loss landscapes have exactly these features, and singular learning theory is the framework that makes the correspondence precise. I am looking to work on that intersection full time — see research for what I mean concretely.

Research areas

Singular learning theory

Degenerate saddles, zero modes and phase transitions as the shared language of strongly coupled field theory and neural network training.

The Yang–Mills vacuum

Analytic calculation of the 3+1d vacuum beyond one loop, including the fate of the ghost sector.

Many-flavor QCD

Semiclassical, continuum treatment of twelve-flavor chiral QCD and the behavior of the Polyakov loop.

Solvable interacting theories

Models that stay exactly solvable at all couplings, and what they reveal about thermal versus entanglement entropy at large N.
