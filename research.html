Singular learning theory

Many models used in machine learning are singular meaning there are zero eigenvalues in the Fisher information matrix. Said otherwise, the Fisher information matrix is degenerate. These degeneracies make a set of optimal weight configurations rather than a single optimal configuration in parameter space. That is, degeneracies generate a wide as opposed to a narrow valley in the loss landscape. The higher number of optimal configurations raises the entropy of the system around the minimum and thus lowers the free energy generating robust minima. Width and depth of the minima both matter.

In analogy to QFT, flat directions in a loss landscape are zero modes which oftentimes signal a symmetry of the system. In physics we know that identifying these symmetries has led to the Standard Model, and the most predictive physics to date. In physics state variables can cause phase transitions from one minimum to another. Such phase transition are responsible for the existence of stable hadronic matter (confinement). Likewise, in learning theory, phase transitions can take place when deep valleys become preferred over wide basins. I am very courious as to the effects of symmetries and phase transitions in learning systems. Nonperturbative field theory and learning theory both remain on the horizon of mathematicial and scientific exploration and, I believe, will share a common synergy.

The Semiclassical Yang–Mills Vacuum

Pure Yang–Mills theory in 3+1 dimensions is a toy model for QCD. It only includes the gauge sector, gluons, which are the QCD analog of photons. Gluons are self-interacting and get trapped at some finite temperature. Like light trapped in a box, the more you squeeze the box, the higher the photons' energy becomes, and the box + photons gains an effective mass. This effective mass + quarks is what leads to protons and neutrons giving about 99% of the observable mass in the universe. I developed nonperturbative analytic methods for Yang-Mills which involve expanding the theory around a classical background. The theoretical foundations of this work go back to Schwinger, Hawking, 't Hooft, Savvidy and others. My works pays close attention and regulate zero modes in the one-loop effective theory, with the use of bosonic ghost terms and zeta-function regularization. Articles found here: arXiv:2407.13042, in “Ghost hunting in the Yang–Mills vacuum” (under review at Nuclear Physics B), and in the Quark Confinement proceedings.

Analytic 12 flavor QCD

With twelve massless quark flavors, QCD sits near the edge of the conformal window, where the theory's infrared behavior is a long-standing open question and lattice studies disagree. Working semiclassically with a background field expansion, Romatschke and I found a vanishing Polyakov loop —an order parameter for confinement — in this theory (Phys. Lett. B 862, 139310).

Exactly solvable interacting theories

Most of what we know about interacting field theories comes from expanding in a small parameter. A small number of models can be solved all couplings, and they are correspondingly valuable as test cases for intrinsicly nonperturbative theories. I constructed a fully solvable model of fermionic interaction in arbitary dimensions with Max Weiner in (JHEP 2023.9:17), and do similar work with scalar theories in N (JHEP 10, 133). More recently, I have explored scalar charges in a self-dual background (arXiv:2607.07938, under review at JHEP).
