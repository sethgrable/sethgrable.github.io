Research

Four threads, running from nonperturbative gauge theory toward the geometry of learning.

Singular learning theory

Statistical models used in machine learning are singular: the map from parameters to functions is not injective, the Fisher information matrix is degenerate, and the set of optimal parameters is a variety rather than a point. Classical asymptotics — the ones that give the BIC, or that treat the posterior as locally Gaussian — simply do not apply. Watanabe's singular learning theory replaces them, and the quantity that takes over the role of parameter counting is a birational invariant of the loss landscape, the real log canonical threshold.

A field theorist recognizes that situation immediately. Flat directions in a loss landscape are zero modes; the degeneracy of a critical point controls the volume of configurations near it and so its weight in the partition function; and a model that reorganizes its internal structure during training is undergoing a phase transition with an order parameter worth identifying. The methods I built for the Yang–Mills vacuum — expanding around a nontrivial saddle, handling the zero-mode sector honestly, tracking where the semiclassical expansion fails — are the same methods this problem asks for.

This is the direction I am actively moving into rather than a body of published work. I am interested in positions and collaborations where degeneracy, phase transitions and interpretability in learning systems are the object of study.

The Yang–Mills vacuum beyond one loop

Pure Yang–Mills theory in 3+1 dimensions is the cleanest setting in which confinement can be posed as a well-defined analytic question. I developed a calculation of the vacuum that goes past one-loop order and stays analytic, and then examined what happens to the ghost sector within it — whether the unphysical modes that gauge fixing introduces leave a trace in vacuum observables. The work appears in arXiv:2407.13042, in “Ghost hunting in the Yang–Mills vacuum” (under review at Nuclear Physics B), and in the Quark Confinement proceedings.

Confinement in many-flavor QCD

With twelve massless quark flavors, QCD sits near the edge of the conformal window, where the theory's infrared behavior is a long-standing open question and lattice studies disagree. Working semiclassically and in the continuum, Paul Romatschke and I found a vanishing Polyakov loop — the order parameter for confinement — in this theory (Phys. Lett. B 862, 139310).

Exactly solvable interacting theories

Most of what we know about interacting field theories comes from expanding in a small parameter. A small number of models can be solved at every coupling, and they are correspondingly valuable as test cases. I constructed a fully solvable model of fermionic interaction in 3+1 dimensions with Michael Weiner (JHEP 2023.9:17), and used all-coupling solvability to compare thermal and entanglement entropy in interacting CFTs at large N (JHEP 10, 133). More recently, with Jorge Barcelona, scalar charges in a self-dual background (arXiv:2607.07938, under review at JHEP).

Earlier work

Before the gauge theory work I studied transport through quantum spin chains coupled to reservoirs using Lindblad master equations — the standard framework for decoherence and noise in qubits — including an out-of-equilibrium quantum analogue of the Onsager reciprocal relations (with Lincoln Carr, Colorado School of Mines). At Case Western I worked with a computer science group on classical algorithms inspired by the HHL quantum linear-system solver (with Shuai Xu), and on a PT-symmetric form of the contact interaction and its condensed-matter applications (with Harsh Mathur).

Methods

Analytic and semi-analytic solution of strongly coupled field theories; semiclassical and saddle-point expansions; open quantum systems; large-scale numerical calculation in Python (NumPy, pandas, PyTorch) and Mathematica, validated against known limits and independent numerical evaluation.
